# TalentPulse Structured Interview and Scoring Guidelines

## 1. Objectives of Structured Interviewing
Structured interviewing provides a calibrated, objective methodology for assessing applicant competencies against empirical job requirements.
Unstructured conversational interviews frequently introduce confirmation bias and subjective impression distortions.
By standardizing question banks, evaluation criteria, and grading rubrics, TalentPulse ensures fair treatment for every candidate.
All hiring managers and interview panellists must complete the mandatory interviewer certification prior to conducting interviews.

## 2. The STAR Assessment Framework
Interview questions must elicit concrete situational evidence rather than hypothetical declarations.
Panellists must direct candidates to articulate their experiences using the Situation, Task, Action, and Result (STAR) framework.
The Situation sets the context, challenges, constraints, and operational environment encountered by the candidate.
The Task defines the candidate's personal responsibility and immediate objectives in that specific scenario.
The Action represents the explicit initiatives, technical implementations, or leadership decisions taken by the candidate.
The Result quantifies the measurable outcome, business impact, organizational learnings, or architectural improvements achieved.
When candidates provide generalized answers, interviewers should prompt specifically for individual contributions and numerical metrics.

## 3. Four-Point Evaluation Rubric
Interviewers evaluate candidate responses across specific competency dimensions using an unambiguous four-point scale.
A rating of 1 corresponds to Strong No Hire, indicating significant gaps in core requirements and foundational reasoning.
A rating of 2 corresponds to No Hire, indicating the candidate meets minimal baselines but falls below team performance standards.
A rating of 3 corresponds to Hire, indicating solid competency alignment, effective execution capability, and positive cultural impact.
A rating of 4 corresponds to Strong Hire, denoting outstanding mastery, exceptional problem decomposition, and role-model leadership.
Neutral or indecisive ratings are prohibited because every interviewer must commit to an actionable recommendation.

## 4. Panellist Calibration and Debrief Procedures
Interview debriefs occur within twenty-four hours of completing the final evaluation round for a candidate slate.
Prior to attending the debrief, all panellists must submit their independent scorecards into the TalentPulse platform.
Panellists are strictly forbidden from viewing peer scorecards or discussing candidate impressions prior to individual submission.
During the debrief session, the talent partner moderates the discussion starting with the technical and practical evaluations.
Where score divergences occur between panellists, members examine specific notes and STAR evidence rather than personal impressions.
The hiring manager retains accountability for the ultimate decision while weighing consensus and minority objections respectfully.

## 5. Mitigating Cognitive Bias during Evaluation
Every interviewer must remain vigilant against pervasive psychological biases throughout the interview process.
Halo and horns effects occur when a single notable trait disproportionately skews the overall assessment of unrelated capabilities.
Similarity bias tempts evaluators to favor candidates sharing similar educational pedigrees, backgrounds, or communication styles.
Recency bias causes panellists to overvalue responses given at the conclusion of an interview while discounting earlier indicators.
To mitigate these biases, interviewers must take contemporaneous written notes and score each rubric dimension independently.

## 6. Prohibited Question Categories
Employment regulations strictly forbid asking questions concerning protected personal characteristics.
Interviewers must never inquire into marital status, family planning, pregnancy, childcare arrangements, or parental obligations.
Questions concerning age, birth dates, high school graduation years, religious beliefs, or political affiliations are illegal.
Inquiries regarding medical history, disabilities, mental wellness, or prior salary history are strictly prohibited under company policy.
If a candidate voluntarily introduces personal matters, interviewers should acknowledge politely and steer discussion back to job competencies.
